By situation

Anaphylaxis rows show the share caught; the others show the share with a false alarm. Darker red is worse. The rules include the literature-review fixes: high histamine counts as exposure for an hour, and a wheeze finding needs wheeze in at least three seconds within a minute. That removed their false alarms on fainting, sensor artifacts and mild allergy. They still miss most episodes where the histamine sensor barely responds.

How this was made

  • A simulator produces 40-minute, one-sample-per-second recordings of what the device's detectors report: heart rate (ECG), breathing rate and apnea (Bio-Z), perfusion index and SpO₂ (PPG), wheeze detections (mic, at the error rates measured on ICBHI), activity, collapse and fall flags (IMUs) and histamine. Each recording has a personal baseline, walking bouts, sensor drop-outs and noise.
  • The rule numbers come from the firmware's own C++ engine and histamine monitor, replayed second by second with the Kconfig defaults.
  • The models see twenty causal inputs: averages, changes from a personal resting baseline, five-minute trends, recent wheeze counts, time since movement, and collapse, fall and sensor-validity flags. An alarm needs the probability above threshold for ten seconds in a row.
  • Training, validation (threshold choice) and test recordings never overlap. The harder-world set uses a different random seed and worse sensors.
  • Because the models learn the simulator's version of each situation, real recordings will score lower. Re-train on data from the patch before trusting any threshold.
